## What it was

My first job after college was at **Capgemini** in Kolkata. I joined a team that supported **Continental**, a global tyre manufacturer.

Continental's internal teams used an **SAP Enterprise Portal** every day. Among other things, the portal had **dashboards** that showed **maintenance data**, helping teams track the health and upkeep of their equipment.

## What I did

I maintained and improved the portal's web pages, working with **HTML**, **CSS**, **JavaScript** and **jQuery**.

### Fixing and polishing the UI

- Fixed **UI bugs** reported by users.
- Made pages **look and work the same** across different browsers.
- Improved **animations and transitions**, so the interface felt smoother and more responsive.

### Dashboards and data

- Connected pages to **REST APIs** to show **live maintenance data**.
- Built **charts and interactive views** for key numbers, so teams could understand the data at a glance.

### Small features

- Added small features with **jQuery** to make common tasks easier for users.

## What I learned

This was my first time working on a large, real-world codebase that many people had built before me. I learned to:

- **Read and understand existing code** before changing it.
- Make **small, careful changes** that don't break anything else.
- Work within a **big team's process**, with reviews and testing before release.

These habits stayed with me for the rest of my career.

## Stack

HTML, CSS, JavaScript, jQuery, REST APIs and SAP Enterprise Portal.
